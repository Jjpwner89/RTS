# Open questions for the creator

Tradeoffs where either answer plays well. The game carries on with the default named in each; answer here or in a questions thread and Realm will adjust.

## Vassals and the Realm screen (2026-11-20)

- **Pause while the Realm screen is open?** It keeps running now, as the map does, so supplies and armies move while you read. Pausing would make it a calm planning screen, at the cost of forgetting to unpause. Default: keeps running (Space still pauses).
- **How often vassals petition you.** Every 10–15 minutes now. More often gives the court more life and more small choices; less keeps the focus on war. Default: 10–15 minutes.
- **What a title costs you** (2026-11-21). *Decided by the orchestrator, 2026-11-21; the creator can override:* sharper. A titled vassal sends half again as many men to your banners but pays a quarter less in dues, their men count double in a faction against you (and twice as many stand in their villages if it rises), and harm done to them is minded half again as much. A real trade: a stronger army and loyalty now, against a more dangerous vassal if they sour. See "Titles" under "Vassals, deeper" in DESIGN_NOTES.

## The militia (2026-11-21)

- **How costly a muster should be.** Played by hand against 38 raiders, the militia always turned the fight, but it cost anything from 3 to 27 villagers (17 of 24 once, when the militia broke). Much of that was the militia standing in front of the keep, fixed since: now a median of 2 to 5 when the retinue goes out to meet the raiders (up to 13), but still 3 to 10 (up to 26) when it is left to hold the keep. Not mustering usually costs the retinue and two or three burned buildings, with few villagers dead, since they shelter. Sturdier militia (say morale 45 instead of 35) would lose fewer people and make the button an easier call; as they are, it is a real gamble. *Decided by the orchestrator, 2026-11-21; the creator can override:* keep the cost. The early game is meant to be brutal (in the style of Prophesy of Pendor), and losing villagers when you give no orders is the lesson. It is made readable instead: the attack message says "Your people will turn out. Lead them, or lose them.", and after a defence the village says how many villagers fell and how (fought with no soldiers beside them, cut down when the militia broke, fell beside your soldiers, or in the levies). See "Militia" in DESIGN_NOTES. (Their numbers belong to the battles session.)

- **Should a fed village send grain to a starving one?** (2026-11-21, round 4). Taken villages starved because their few hands stood at the wrong jobs; that is fixed by job order alone, so none is needed. Carts of grain between your villages (a fed place sending its spare to a hungry one, slowly, on the roads, where raiders could catch them) would make conquest softer and give the map a supply chain to guard. It would also loosen the food tension that makes farms matter. Default: not built.

## The journey (2026-11-21)

- **Buying a free place's fealty** (Count and above). The price now is 6 gold a villager and 10 a man of the garrison, half again for a market, abbey or castle: from 320 gold for a poor village with 4 men, to 860–1,050 for an abbey or market and 1,600–1,700 for a castle, against about 3,800 gold in the Count test save. Cheaper makes gold the main road to growth; dearer keeps the sword the main road and gold the shortcut. Default: as built, with the sword the main road.
- **An early fealty path.** A Reeve might swear to a great house (as in Mount & Blade) for protection and a share of its wars, then break away when strong enough. It would add a second way up, at the cost of a new set of rules for being someone's vassal. Default: not built; you start as your own lord.
- **Great vassals: who picks them, and how hard they press** (built 2026-11-21). You make a duke yourself with the raise button, and nobody is raised on their own. The claim is the price: refused at the press, a claim on your own place costs −30, and one on a vassal's place starts a seizure and a feud. If raising happened on its own at the crowning, the King stage would bring trouble by itself; as built, the trouble only comes if you choose the trade. Default: as built, by your hand.
