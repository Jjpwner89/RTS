Welcome to Hearth and Host

You are the reeve of Ashford, a small village with twenty men. To begin:

  1. Double-click Ashford on the map (it glows) to go into your village.
  2. Build a woodcutter and a farm: pick one on the bar at the bottom, then click to place it. Your people need food first.
  3. Space pauses the game whenever you want to think; you can still build while it is paused. Esc opens the menu, where you can save.

The Goals panel on the left names the next step, and the thing to press glows. How everything works is under ? How to play at any time.

Hearth and Host: the campaign

You are the reeve of Ashford, one village in the south with twenty men. Five great houses hold the rest of the realm, and the old royal city of Kingsbridge stands empty of a king at its heart.

World map: click a place or army to select it, double-click a village of yours to go there. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, middle-drag or Q / E to turn. Fog hides what you have not seen.

Armies: in a village, select regiments and press March out, or muster the whole garrison from the map. Right-click a place to march there. You fight for a place in the place itself: storm it, burn what you like, and what is left standing is yours. Armies meeting on the road fight in the open.

Building: pick a building and click to place it; it stays in hand for more, until you right-click or press Esc. B then a letter picks one (shown on each button).

Villages keep working while you are away; if one is attacked, its garrison fights on its own. There you can muster the militia: every villager turns out as a weak soldier until the attack is over (the governor does it for you only when the keep itself is assaulted by more men than you have). Other villages do the same when you assault them with more men than they have under arms, so bring enough to outnumber their people too. Hauling: goods stay where they are made until haulers carry them, and arms must reach a barracks before a levy can be raised.

People are the limit at the start: every building wants hands, and you begin with little wood and stone. Newcomers settle while there is food to spare, a good mood and a good name (a chapel, market, well and quiet years help; raids and cold hearths hurt); houses only set how many can live there. Every house burns firewood. Hunting grounds run out and breed back slowly, and a new farm takes a few minutes to bring in its first crop. Now and then a group of settlers asks to stay.

Mood: food, bread, wells and chapels lift it; high taxes, crowding, cold and their dead weigh on it. A wretched village loses families, then revolts.

Upgrades add work places (level 2 needs stone and planks, level 3 gold and iron); − / + on a building sets how many work there. Stone and ore run out: hover a deposit to see what is left. Paved roads (Village tab) speed everyone who walks on them. A building that is stuck says why above it. ½× slows the game down.

Armies eat: they carry four minutes of food and refill near your places. Far from home, send a supply wagon from the army panel, or they desert. Call the banners to gather every village's men in one place.

Houses raid before they march, and their heralds warn you when a host gathers. Click a house to send gifts, make peace, ally or marry into it. Taxes bring in gold.

Every place has real buildings, people and stores, poor or rich; what you storm is what is there, and its coin is yours. Select a place and send a spy to see its stores, buildings and garrison.

Rising in rank opens new tools. From Lord: grant a village to a vassal, who runs it, pays you dues and sends men to your banners; keep their opinion up (the Realm screen shows the reasons). Town templates on the build bar's Towns tab lay out a block of buildings at once. From Count: a knights' hall trains men-at-arms and knights from veterans who came home from war.

Sellsword companies wait at the market towns; hire them at your keep. They want wages every minute, and unpaid they desert, often as bandits.

Gold pays your knights and men-at-arms every minute, buys iron, horses, grain, mail and swords at market towns (dearer with every lot, carted home), holds feasts and raises a great keep (at the keep), builds stone walls and hires masons (Defences).

Outlaws come first: twice in the first quarter hour (not in a gentle game) a small band comes out of the woods for your stores. Keep your retinue at home, and when the band reaches the village, select the retinue and right-click the outlaws. Beaten off, they take nothing; let them be, and they leave with a share of your food, wood and gold.

Rival houses start stronger than you, but first settle their own lands, then reach further, and at last play to win. Close the gap on one and it turns on you sooner. Click a house to see what it is about.

Realm (top bar, or Tab) brings the realm together. Realm: what needs you, your council's advice, your vassals' mood and the great houses. Villages: every place side by side (click a column to sort). Supplies: send a wagon of food or building goods from one village to another, see who is short and what is on the road. Vassals: each one's opinion and why, what they want, their power, and everything you can do with them: gifts and honours, dues and levies, council seats, granting or taking back land, demanding gold, calling them to arms, judging feuds and arrest. Watch the warnings: a resentful vassal stops paying, and the discontented band into a faction that rises if refused. Council: a steward, a marshal and an envoy, each best with the right knack. Houses and Treasury: your ties, and where the gold comes from and goes.

Needs you, bottom left of the map, lists what wants your attention, worst first. Click a line to look at it. In a village, a number on the World map button counts the serious ones.

Win by being crowned: hold Kingsbridge and half the realm, or win every great house to back your claim. M returns to the map from a village, and Space pauses anywhere. The game saves itself every two minutes; Continue on the menu. Esc (when it has nothing else to close) or the Menu button opens the game menu: resume, save, sound, quit.

Sound: 🔊 in the top bar sets the volume or mutes. Credits for the music, sound and models.
